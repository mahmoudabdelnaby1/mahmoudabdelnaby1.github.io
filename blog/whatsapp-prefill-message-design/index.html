Design a WhatsApp Prefill Message That Helps Without Sharing Private Data

Draft a short, optional message starter that gives a business useful context while leaving control with the visitor.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·3 min read·العربية

A prefilled WhatsApp message should save typing while leaving the visitor in control. It is only a draft on the visitor’s device; the business has not received an inquiry until the person sends it and the team can access it.

Design the text around one useful context

Include only details the visitor deliberately selected and that help route the request: service type, market or district, and a short question. Keep the message editable. Do not add a phone number, order value, health detail, or hidden identifier to the message or URL. Avoid wording that claims a booking or submission has already occurred.

Illustrative draft: “Hello, I’d like to ask about [service]. My area is [district]. Could you confirm availability and the next step?” The bracketed values should be optional selections, not data silently inferred by the site.

Test the handoff, not just the button

On a real phone, check that Arabic characters and right-to-left text survive the link, spaces and punctuation display correctly, the right WhatsApp destination opens, and the user can edit before sending. Test both language routes and one empty optional field. Confirm the fallback contact method if WhatsApp is not installed or opens the wrong account.

Measure button clicks, chats opened, messages sent, replies, qualified inquiries, and outcomes as separate events. A click event cannot establish that a message was sent. Tell the team what campaign context may be recorded and limit retention to what is useful and permitted.
