Write a Positioning Brief Before Choosing Channels

Clarify audience, problem, difference and evidence before spreading effort across platforms.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·2 min read·العربية

A positioning brief should help a buyer understand why this offer fits their situation and what evidence supports the difference. “Best quality and great service” cannot guide a channel or a creative decision.

Make four choices explicit

  1. Audience in a decision moment: name the person and the situation, not “everyone in MENA.”
  2. Problem or desired progress: describe what is hard today in the buyer’s own terms.
  3. Alternative: include doing nothing, an internal workaround, or a competitor—not only a product category.
  4. Difference with proof: say what the offer does differently and what verifiable evidence makes that believable.

Then write one sentence: “For [specific buyer] facing [situation], [offer] helps them [progress] through [distinct mechanism], supported by [evidence].” Delete the bracketed phrase if you cannot substantiate it.

Pressure-test with a real alternative

Suppose an analytics consultancy says it helps ecommerce teams “make better decisions.” Ask what a team currently does instead—export reports manually, trust platform conversions, or wait for a monthly dashboard. A meaningful position might focus on connecting inquiry stages to operational outcomes, but only if the service actually implements and checks that chain. If every competitor could claim the same difference, it is not distinctive yet.

Use the brief to choose channels

The brief is a filter: which audience question belongs in search, which process benefits from a demonstration, and which decision needs a direct conversation? Do not select channels before the buyer task and proof are clear. Revisit the brief when customer interviews or sales objections contradict the assumed problem.
