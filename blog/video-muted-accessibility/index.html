Make Video Ads Understandable With Sound Off

Keep the message accessible when a viewer watches muted or needs captions.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·2 min read·العربية

A sound-off viewer should still understand the offer, proof, and next step. Captions are part of the message design, not an afterthought added after the edit is locked.

Run a silent comprehension check

Watch the video muted on a phone. At each beat, ask: can the viewer identify the problem, understand what is being demonstrated, and read the action before the shot changes? If voice-over carries essential information, add accurate captions or move the information into the visual.

For Arabic and bilingual video, check shaping, right-to-left order, line breaks, contrast, and text safe areas. Do not place a price, disclaimer, or call to action under interface controls. Avoid captions that cover the product demonstration or flash too quickly to read. Include meaningful sound cues only when they add context; do not rely on color alone to show a state change.

Example acceptance checklist

  • The first screen states or shows the subject without audio.
  • Captions match the spoken words and are proofread in the target register.
  • Each frame has one reading priority.
  • Price, eligibility, and next action remain visible long enough to understand.
  • The final action label matches the destination button or page.

Have someone who did not edit the video summarize it after a silent view. If their summary misses a limitation or the next step, revise the visual hierarchy rather than adding more tiny text.
