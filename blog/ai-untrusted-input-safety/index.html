Protect Marketing AI Workflows From Untrusted Instructions

Keep customer content and retrieved pages from overriding approved workflow rules.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·3 min read·العربية

A page, form, or customer message can contain text that looks like an instruction to an AI system. The workflow must treat that material as input data, not authority to reveal information or take a separate action.

Separate the instruction from the content

Keep trusted system rules, approved sources, user-supplied material, and tool permissions in separate fields. Tell the model what it may do with the supplied material—for example, summarize or classify it—and what it may never do, such as export contacts, send messages, change a campaign, or reveal hidden instructions.

Test with adversarial examples:

  • a webpage asks the model to ignore its assigned task;
  • a form includes a demand to expose another customer’s details;
  • a retrieved document contains contradictory instructions;
  • an Arabic/English mixed message hides an action request in the other language.

The expected result is to process the content for the authorized task, flag the hostile instruction where relevant, and avoid the unauthorized action. Do not put credentials or broad account access in a workflow that only needs to summarize text.

Bound the tools and review the output

Give read-only access where possible, require human approval for external actions, and validate the structured output against a schema. Log the input source, rule version, blocked action, and reviewer without retaining unnecessary personal content. Test after changing the model, prompt, tools, or retrieval sources.

A successful small test does not prove the workflow is immune to prompt injection. Keep the action surface narrow and provide a manual fallback.
