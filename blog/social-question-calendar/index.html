Build a Social Calendar Around Audience Questions

Give every post a job grounded in a real audience question.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·7 min read·العربية

A social calendar should start with a question a real audience asks, then choose the format that helps answer it. Filling dates first usually produces a schedule with no reader job.

Turn one question into a useful week

Collect questions from sales calls, support messages, comments, search queries, and product use. Remove private details, merge true duplicates, and label each question by the decision behind it: learn, compare, verify, or act. Choose one question for the week and give each post a different job.

Post role Reader job Example for a MENA service business
Explain Understand a term or process A short Arabic explainer of what an initial site assessment includes
Demonstrate See a task completed A phone video showing the steps, with captions
Resolve doubt Check a real limitation A post clarifying which areas are served and how to confirm coverage
Invite action Take one next step A booking link that names what information is needed

These are format ideas, not a recommendation to publish all four every week. Use the channel and audience evidence you actually have.

Make each post earn its place

Write the answer before writing the hook. The hook must set up the answer, not promise a result the post cannot prove. If the question is market-specific, name the country or service conditions; do not call an example “for MENA” when it applies only to one market. Adapt Egyptian colloquial, Gulf colloquial, and neutral Arabic deliberately, rather than mixing registers in one sentence.

At the end of the week, review saves, substantive replies, profile visits, and completed next actions against the post’s goal. Low reach can mean distribution trouble; high reach with no useful response can mean a mismatch in task or audience. These signals do not prove sales impact on their own.

Use the calendar to answer, then listen

For every audience question, choose one evidence format and one response prompt that can reveal what remains unclear. Group posts into a sequence only when the later item genuinely depends on the earlier one. Reserve space to respond to recurring questions and update a post when answers expose a missing step. Track saves, meaningful replies, and qualified next actions by post purpose; do not compare a discovery clip and a how-to carousel with the same success measure. The calendar should change when the audience teaches you something, not merely when a date arrives.
