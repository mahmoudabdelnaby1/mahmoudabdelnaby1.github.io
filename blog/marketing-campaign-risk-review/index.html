Run a Campaign Risk Review Before Launch

Catch broken destinations, unverified claims, missing owners and measurement gaps before spend starts.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·6 min read·العربية

A campaign preflight should identify who can stop launch and what evidence clears each risk. A checklist with no owner becomes a set of boxes everyone assumes someone else checked.

Review six launch surfaces

Offer: Is the price, availability, eligibility, and exclusion accurate in every language?

Destination: Does each ad open the intended country, product, and language page on mobile?

Claims: Can the team support every performance, comparison, testimonial, and urgency statement? Are permissions recorded?

Operations: Is inventory or service capacity ready? Is a person assigned to inquiries, including outside standard hours if promised?

Measurement: Are click, message, qualified request, order, delivery, and refund events defined and tested separately?

Market and platform: Has an owner checked current rules and requirements from primary sources? Record date and market; do not rely on a copied checklist where regulations may differ.

Assign launch states

For each risk, mark clear, blocked, or accepted with a named owner and mitigation. Legal, safety, privacy, and unverified-claim blockers do not become acceptable because a deadline is close. Test one real click path per language and device class. Save the final creative, destination, tracking version, approver, and launch time together.

Use a post-launch check

Within the first operating window, confirm the ad is active, destination loads, forms or chats arrive, and the assigned team can respond. Pause only the affected path if one control fails; preserve evidence before changing settings. A green platform status does not prove the full customer journey works.

Additional applied format: Decision map: Run a Campaign Risk Review Before Launch

Start with the decision in this article: A campaign preflight should identify who can stop launch and what evidence clears each risk.

If the current state cannot be verified: collect the missing record or fact first, and log what remains unknown.

If the evidence confirms the problem is in scope: take one action: Offer: Is the price, availability, eligibility, and exclusion accurate in every language? Keep its source, date, and owner.

If the evidence does not confirm it: do not apply the fix because the case merely looks similar; check the nearest alternative in the source material: Operations: Is inventory or service capacity ready?

Before deciding: compare baseline and outcome using the same source, and log exceptions or a quality guardrail. If measurement is unavailable, pause the conclusion and assign evidence collection.
