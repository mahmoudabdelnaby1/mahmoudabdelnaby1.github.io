Create a Community Response Guide for Social Teams

Help a team answer accurately, protect privacy and route issues to someone who can resolve them.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·8 min read·العربية

A response guide is useful when it helps a teammate choose the next safe action—not when it gives everyone the same polished sentence. Replies should match the issue, the channel, and the level of risk.

Route before replying

Create four routes and assign an owner to each: routine question; service or order issue; complaint or sensitive personal detail; threat, safety issue, or legal request. The responder should identify the route, acknowledge what can be confirmed, and take the conversation to a private channel only when account details are needed. Never ask someone to post a phone number, order ID, health detail, or payment proof publicly.

Public reply example: “We can check the service area for you. Please use the private contact link in our profile; don’t share your phone number here.” This acknowledges the task without pretending the team has already resolved it.

Give the team a decision card

For each route, specify: what the responder may answer from an approved source; what they must not promise; the escalation owner; the response-time expectation the business has actually approved; and how to record the case without copying unnecessary personal data. If the business cannot support a response-time promise, don’t invent one in the guide.

For an Arabic account, prepare natural local responses rather than translating the English script word for word. Keep the same policy and next step across languages. If a complaint alleges harm or a regulated service issue, follow the organization’s approved escalation process instead of improvising a public statement.

Review for resolution, not activity

Track issue type, first response, route, escalation, and whether the issue was resolved—not just number of replies. Sample conversations for accuracy and privacy, then update the card when a repeated question exposes a missing policy. Hide or remove a public detail only according to platform controls and the organization’s rules; preserve an internal record needed for follow-up.
