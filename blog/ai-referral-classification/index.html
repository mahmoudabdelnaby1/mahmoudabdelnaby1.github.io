Classify AI Referrals Carefully in Analytics

Separate known referrers, tagged campaigns and unattributed traffic without overclaiming source.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·3 min read·العربية

AI referral reporting is a classification problem with missing data. A session with no referrer is not proof that a person arrived from an AI assistant.

Use three attribution buckets

  1. Identified referrer: the analytics system records a known assistant domain or app referral. Preserve the raw source and landing page.
  2. Tagged campaign: a tracked link carries campaign parameters. Report it as tagged traffic, even if the user later switches apps.
  3. Unattributed or unknown: direct, stripped, private-share, app, and missing-referrer visits that cannot be reliably assigned. Keep them unknown.

Maintain an allowlist of observed referrers with the date and reason each was added. Verify actual traffic before adding a new pattern. Do not label every “direct” session or branded search as AI traffic.

Keep the measurement chain honest

Separate sessions, engaged visits, contact clicks, messages sent, qualified inquiries, and confirmed outcomes. If a user copies an answer into a private chat and then types the site URL, the origin may be invisible; no regex can restore evidence that was never transmitted. A model mention in a manual prompt check is also not an analytics referral.

Review source/medium and landing pages, test channel rules on known examples, and keep a change log when adding patterns. Compare identified AI referrals separately from unknown traffic. Explain coverage limits in every report and avoid presenting a regex as a complete measurement solution.
