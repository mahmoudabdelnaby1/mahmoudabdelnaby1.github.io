Decide Whether an A/B Test Has Enough Evidence

Choose a hypothesis, primary measure and stopping rule before opening a test.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·7 min read·العربية

An A/B test needs a decision rule before it needs a winner. If the team changes the stopping date after seeing early results, the test may reward noise or a calendar effect.

Write the protocol before launch

Document the hypothesis, one primary outcome, guardrails, audience, variants, allocation method, minimum run conditions, and stop rule. Choose an outcome close to the business decision—qualified inquiries or completed bookings, for example—when volume and measurement allow. If the event is too rare, select a valid leading indicator and state that the business outcome remains unproven.

Check the evidence in order

  • Is assignment stable and are users seeing the intended variant?
  • Is tracking firing once and using the same definition for both groups?
  • Did stock, price, campaign mix, device mix, or sales follow-up change during the test?
  • Did the observation window include the full weekly pattern and relevant local dates?
  • Is there enough data for the planned comparison, or should the result be labeled inconclusive?

In MENA, do not assume every audience shares one weekend, payday, or seasonal pattern. Note the country and date range; avoid comparing a Ramadan promotion directly with an ordinary month without a reasoned design.

A small difference after three days is not a universal winner rule. Use a suitable statistical method or a transparent practical threshold agreed before the test; if uncertain, get a measurement specialist to review it. Stop for safety, broken tracking, or business constraints—not because one chart briefly looks favorable.
