Clarify an Offer Before Redesigning the Whole Page

Resolve uncertainty about price, eligibility, inclusions and next steps before cosmetic changes.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·6 min read·العربية

A redesign cannot fix an offer a visitor cannot explain. Before changing colors or layout, make the service, scope, eligibility, price basis, and next step clear enough to repeat.

Use the five-line offer test

Ask a person unfamiliar with the page to answer:

  1. What is being offered?
  2. Who is it for—and who is not eligible?
  3. What is included and excluded?
  4. What affects the price, and when will the visitor learn it?
  5. What happens after they contact or book?

If answers differ from the business’s intended terms, fix the wording and evidence first. For a service whose price depends on inspection, say which factors change the quote and whether the first assessment costs anything. Do not display a fake “from” price without a real qualifying basis.

Example before the form

Weak: “Contact us for premium solutions.”

Clearer: “Request a site assessment for offices in the listed Cairo districts. We confirm coverage and the assessment fee before scheduling. After you send the form, the service team reviews the location and replies with available times.”

The second version is only suitable if the business can verify and deliver those terms.

Put proof next to the claim it supports, and keep the CTA specific: check coverage, request a quote, or book an assessment. State response expectations only when the team can meet them. If the offer varies by country, make that distinction visible before the visitor submits personal details.

Measure whether visitors understand the offer and complete the intended qualified step—not just button clicks. Ask sales which question still appears repeatedly; that is often a better clarity signal than a redesign opinion.
