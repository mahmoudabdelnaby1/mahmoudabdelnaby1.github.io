Is X Still Useful for Marketing in Saudi Arabia? Test the Channel Against Its Job

A Saudi-market framework for comparing X, TikTok, and Snapchat by audience need, content fit, measurable next step, and service capacity.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·7 min read·العربية

“Does X still sell in Saudi Arabia, or has everyone moved to TikTok and Snapchat?” combines three different questions: whether an audience is present, whether a channel fits a purchase decision, and whether a business can verify that a post or ad contributed to a commercial result. Platform popularity and follower counts do not answer those questions for a specific offer. There is no sound basis for claiming that an entire market has moved from one platform to another without current, relevant evidence.

Start with the customer’s task. Do people need a concise explanation, a product demonstration, a conversation around a topic, or a clear route to request or buy? Then compare channels where the business can create suitable content and measure what happens next. X lists campaign objectives such as reach, video views, engagement, and website conversions; TikTok Ads and Snapchat for Business describe their own objective groupings. Options can vary by account and market, so check the current account before planning. A selected objective describes what the ad system is optimizing for; it does not guarantee a qualified inquiry or sale. X campaign objectives · TikTok Ads objectives · Snapchat ad objectives

Start with the audience’s task, not app popularity

Define the audience in the Saudi market or service area: location, language, need, and decision stage. Do not apply nationwide data to one city or sector without evidence. Use customer questions, relevant search queries, and privacy-safe inquiry themes to learn what people are trying to understand. Then ask which format can help: a sequence of explanations, a short demonstration, a conversation, or a direct request path.

Give each channel a testable role. One might be tested for explaining a topic or participating in a relevant conversation; another for visual product use; another for a brief demonstration or offer. These are hypotheses, not fixed truths about every platform or its users. Check fit using a specific account, audience, content, and next step.

Audience need What to test Suitable evidence
Understand an idea or decision A concise explanation or sequence with steps and limitations Meaningful completion, relevant follow-up questions, or next-page visits
See how a product or service works A demonstration, substantiated use case, or documented process Visits to a relevant page followed by a qualified action
Explore an option or topic A useful contribution to a relevant discussion Relevant replies and audience fit, not likes alone
Take an action A clear page, form, or chat route Requests that meet a defined qualification rule and can be verified

Keep organic publishing separate from paid distribution

You may publish an idea organically and promote a version of it, but the results are not directly comparable by default. Organic distribution and a paid campaign use different delivery, settings, objectives, and reporting. Record the post type, creative version, audience and market, destination, and stated objective.

For advertising, select an objective that matches the action you need and can measure. Do not choose video views and later present views as inquiries. Confirm that the landing page or chat works in the relevant language and that the offer, price, availability, and service coverage match the audience.

Test channel fit with a small, interpretable comparison

Do not launch three unrelated campaigns and compare raw totals. Create channel-appropriate versions that preserve the core offer and claim, then document changes in hook, length, visuals, and call to action. Avoid copying one post verbatim when the channel requires different context or pacing.

Decide what the test should teach you: can the content reach a suitable audience, hold attention, prompt a useful next step, or generate a qualified request? Set a spending and time boundary based on business risk and capacity. Use the same qualification rule and response process where possible. No universal number of days guarantees a meaningful result for every business.

Field experiment thread: Test channel fit in Saudi Arabia

This is a proposed test, not a published result or performance promise.

1/ Choose one audience need, a specific segment, and a service area. Write the rule that makes an inquiry qualified.

2/ Select two or three channels based on content format and the measurable next action—not an assumption that a trending platform is best.

3/ Prepare a suitable version for each channel while preserving the offer and core information. Record differences in hook, length, and call to action.

4/ Verify the account, campaign objective, destination, links, and landing page before launch. Do not assume every option is available to every account.

5/ Measure attention or viewing separately from visits, conversations, qualified inquiries, and commercial outcomes in their respective systems.

6/ Compare cost, request quality, service capacity, and production effort. If budgets, audiences, or measurement differ, state the limits and do not claim causal superiority.

Tool-neutral prompt pack: Match a channel to the audience question

Use aggregated or redacted research and verify account options manually.

“The audience is [description and service area], the need is [question], the offer is [verified offer], and the measurable goal is [outcome]. Compare a test across [X, TikTok, Snapchat] using format fit, click or chat path, service availability, measurement quality, production effort, and account-specific risks. Use the attached current list of objectives from each account. Do not claim a platform guarantees visibility or sales. Propose one testable hypothesis and native content concept per channel, an attention metric and a qualified-inquiry metric, a spending boundary, and a decision rule. Flag every feature or audience claim that needs current verification.”

Do not accept an automated guess about audience behavior without supporting data. Check the live account settings, destination page, and actual records.

Short-video script: Do not choose a channel by view count

Opening: “X, TikTok, or Snapchat? Start with what the customer needs.”

Scene 1: Show a real audience question. “Do they need an explanation, a demonstration, or a clear way to request?”

Scene 2: Show three different executions of one offer. “Adapt the format while keeping the promise and facts consistent.”

Scene 3: Show a measurement path. “A view, a visit, and a qualified inquiry are different stages.”

Close: “Test the channel against the goal and your ability to serve the request. No platform guarantees reach.”

Educational carousel: A practical way to choose a channel

  1. Define the need: what is the audience trying to do?
  2. Choose a format: explanation, discussion, video, or product use.
  3. Map the next step: where does a person go after watching?
  4. Check market fit: are the offer and service available in that location?
  5. Separate organic and paid: they use different delivery and measurement.
  6. Write the goal: what action counts as success?
  7. Measure quality: classify inquiries by a published rule.
  8. Check capacity: can the team create and respond consistently?

Stop-doing swipe file: Replace platform generalizations

Stop: “Everyone in Saudi Arabia is on one platform.”

Use: “We are testing this channel with [audience and area] because [need-related reason]. We have not measured its share of the whole market.”

Stop: “The video got views, so it generated sales.”

Use: “The content recorded [view definition], the destination recorded [visits], and [count] requests met [qualification rule].”

Stop: “The same ad works everywhere.”

Use: “We preserved the offer and adapted [format, opening, and call to action], documenting the differences.”

Stop: “The campaign objective guarantees customers.”

Use: “The objective sets the platform’s optimization direction; we measure request quality and business results separately.”

Hook–Value–Template: Choose a channel from a real question

Hook: “Which channel helps this audience take the next useful step?”

Value: “Channel choice starts with the audience need, format, measurement path, and service capacity—not a popularity ranking or a promise of reach.”

Template: “We serve [audience and area] seeking [need]. We will test [channels] with [formats] while preserving [offer and facts]. The next action is [action], and a qualified inquiry means [rule]. We will measure [attention] separately from [visits], [inquiries], and [verified outcomes]. Production and response capacity are [resources], and the spending boundary is [limit]. We review on [date] and decide using [criteria], documenting [limitations].”
