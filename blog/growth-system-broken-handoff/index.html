PRACTICAL GUIDE · Growth strategy

Before Adding a Marketing Channel, Find the Broken Handoff

Diagnose whether growth is constrained by discovery, offer clarity, conversion, follow-up, or measurement.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·3 min read·العربية

When growth slows, the default response is often to add a channel, raise spend, or publish more. Those actions increase activity; they do not tell us where the customer journey is failing.

Start with one verifiable business outcome: a completed purchase, a qualified inquiry, a confirmed booking, or a retained customer. Define the evidence and the team responsible for recording it. Then map the handoffs that lead to it.

Stage Diagnostic question Useful evidence
Discovery Are the intended people finding us? Search queries, reach by market, referral source
Understanding Can visitors explain the offer and next step? Page behavior, sales questions, usability feedback
Action Can they complete the action without friction? Form completion, checkout steps, contact intent
Follow-up Does the request reach an owner promptly? Delivery status, response time, CRM stage
Outcome Did the business result actually happen? Order, booking, attendance, renewal, close record

Choose the narrowest failed transition, not the most fashionable channel. If relevant visitors arrive but leave before understanding the offer, test the page explanation before buying more reach. If inquiries arrive but are never answered, fix ownership and response workflow before increasing lead volume.

For each proposed change, name the audience, the exact explanation to change, the signal expected to move, and why. Set a success threshold, observation window, and guardrail such as lead quality or cancellations. This keeps teams from calling more clicks “growth.”

A MENA diagnostic: include the handoff after the click

In markets where a conversation may move from a social post to WhatsApp and then to a human sales team, add those handoffs to the map. Preserve campaign context with a suitable reference or tagged destination, define which locations are serviceable, and assign a real owner for replies. A click, an opened chat, and an in-coverage appointment request are different events. Do not combine them into one “lead” number.

Trace the failure, not the symptom

Take one real journey and follow a single test record from first touch to the final business outcome. At each handoff, write the sender, receiver, expected payload, acknowledgement, and fallback owner. A missing acknowledgement is a process defect even when the dashboard records a conversion. For example, if a form submission reaches a shared inbox but no named person owns it, the bottleneck is routing and response—not acquisition. Run the same trace on a second device and language before changing channel spend.
