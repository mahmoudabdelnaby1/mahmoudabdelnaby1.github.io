Storyboard a Short Video Around One Audience Problem

Give each scene one job and connect the opening, proof and next step.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·7 min read·العربية

A short storyboard should make one audience problem visible, show a credible step toward solving it, and name the next action. If every shot says something different, the viewer cannot tell what the ad is about.

Build three beats around one task

Beat Viewer should learn Example: booking a service
Opening Recognize a specific problem A visitor is unsure whether the service covers their district
Proof/process See one verifiable step Show the coverage selector and what information is needed
Next action Know exactly what to do “Check your area” links to the real coverage page

This storyboard does not claim the service is available everywhere or guarantee a booking. The final screen should show the actual destination and any eligibility condition.

Protect the production from drift

Write the exact on-screen text, voice-over, visual, duration target, and evidence source for each beat. For Arabic, specify whether the voice is Egyptian, Gulf, or neutral MSA; add reviewed subtitles and right-to-left proofing for every graphics frame. Keep prices, locations, and availability current. If a claim cannot be shown or verified, remove it instead of dressing it up with stock footage.

Review the storyboard on a phone-sized preview before filming. Ask a person unfamiliar with the brief to explain the offer and next step after one viewing. If they cannot, simplify the shot sequence.

30-second storyboard: show the evidence, not a promise

0–3 sec

Visual: a dashboard says “12 leads.” Voice: “How many became real requests?”

3–9 sec

Show four labels: click, chat opened, reply, qualified request.

9–20 sec

Explain the distinction using one hypothetical contact; label the scenario illustrative.

20–27 sec

Show the weekly reconciliation sheet and point to the first gap.

27–30 sec

CTA: “Save the four states before your next report.”
