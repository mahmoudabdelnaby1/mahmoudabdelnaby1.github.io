Plan Local Landing Pages Without Thin City Swaps

Create a local page only when verified service details make it meaningfully more useful to people in that place.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·7 min read·العربية

A location page should help a person make a market-specific decision. Replacing the city name in one national service page does not create local usefulness. Before drafting, test whether the business really serves that location and whether the answer changes there.

Apply the four-gate page test

A local page is eligible only when all four questions have a defensible answer:

  1. Coverage: Can the business deliver or provide the service there today? Name the source and owner who confirms it.
  2. Operational difference: Do availability, timing, service process, language, fees, or eligibility differ by location?
  3. Local proof: Is there a real, permissioned example, team, facility, or service detail tied to this area? Never borrow another city’s proof.
  4. Distinct next step: Can the visitor check availability, contact the right team, or complete a location-specific action on this page?

If the only difference is the place name, retain a single regional page with a coverage selector. Do not create thin doorway pages that funnel users to the same generic destination. Choose the right page architecture

Use one national service page plus a location selector when the offer and process are the same. Create separate city pages only when the service, eligibility, operational instructions, or proof genuinely changes and the page can answer that difference in full. A regional team page may be more honest than dozens of near-identical neighborhood pages.

Keep the page useful beyond its opening paragraph: explain coverage boundaries, response route, prerequisites, what is not served, and how a visitor can confirm a request. Avoid invented addresses, local testimonials, maps, or “near me” wording that does not match operations.

Review after launch

Assign an owner and review date to coverage, hours, staff, pricing, and local proof. Track organic queries by location, successful local actions, out-of-area inquiries, and complaints about unavailable service. If no distinct audience task or evidence remains, merge the page into the regional hub and update internal links instead of maintaining a shell.

Treat page eligibility as an operational promise

A location page should help someone make a local decision that a regional page cannot answer. Before drafting, verify a real service area, a local difference in availability or process, evidence that can be maintained, and a distinct next step. If any gate fails, strengthen the regional page instead. Keep a source owner and review date beside every local claim—hours, coverage, delivery, staff, or contact details. This turns the anti-doorway rule into an editorial control: unsupported city variants stay unpublished, while genuine service differences receive specific explanations.

Doorway-page boundary

A city page needs a distinct user benefit and real local information. Google identifies substantially similar city pages that funnel visitors elsewhere as doorway abuse. Google spam policies.
