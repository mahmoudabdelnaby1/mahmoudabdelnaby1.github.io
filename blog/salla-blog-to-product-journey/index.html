From a Salla Blog Post to a Product Page: Make the Next Step Useful

A practical way to connect a Salla store article to a relevant product page, choose clear anchor text, and measure clicks without claiming sales or rankings.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·8 min read·العربية

A product link should help readers act on an answer the article has already given them. Start with the reader's question, then point to one relevant next step. A useful internal link can help visitors move through a store and help search engines understand how pages relate; it cannot guarantee a purchase or a higher ranking.

Salla's Store Blog documentation describes publishing product-related articles, managing categories, and enabling the blog in store navigation. Google recommends making important ecommerce pages discoverable through crawlable links and using meaningful link text. These are implementation references, not evidence that a particular link will rank or sell. Salla: Manage your store blog, Salla: Write a new blog post, and Google: Ecommerce site structure.

Map the reader's question to a decision

Write down what the reader came to understand and what decision may reasonably follow. Choose a destination because it answers that next question, not simply because it has the highest margin.

Reader intent Useful article Possible next page Relevance check
Diagnose a problem or choose a criterion Troubleshooting guide or comparison Matching product or relevant category Does it meet the criteria explained in the article?
Learn how to use an item Instructions or FAQ The exact item discussed Can the shopper confirm compatibility before ordering?
Compare alternatives A bounded comparison with stated factors Pages for the options actually reviewed Are differences and limitations represented fairly?
Maintain an existing product Care or maintenance guide Compatible accessory or replacement Is the additional item necessary to complete the task?

If no product fits, do not invent a commercial destination. Leave the article without a sales link or offer another guide that completes the reader's task.

Choose a destination that solves the next step

Open the destination and confirm that it is the intended product or category page, works on mobile, and presents current price, availability, and compatibility details. A link to a generic homepage is rarely helpful when the article names a specific item. If a product is unavailable, remove the link or direct readers to a genuinely comparable alternative while explaining the difference. Follow the store's own availability and substitution policies.

Use link text that describes what is on the destination page. For example: “Check the dimensions of the [Model X phone stand]” or “Compare [care kits for this material].” Avoid repeating “Buy now” throughout an informational article, and replace vague “click here” text with a clear destination where possible. Google's link guidance explains the value of crawlable links and descriptive anchor text. Google: Crawlable links.

Place the link where it supports understanding

Add the link after explaining the criterion that makes the destination relevant, rather than before the reader knows why it is there. State why the item fits, what limitation matters, and what the shopper should verify.

Illustrative example: In a guide to choosing a water filter, explain the differences between filter types and the maintenance criteria first. Then link to a product only if its published specifications match those criteria. Do not call it “the best” or claim it guarantees healthy water without suitable evidence and permission to make that claim.

A travel-packing article could link to a particular bag after explaining its capacity and dimensions. State the dimensions accurately and ask readers to check their carrier's current rules; do not guarantee that every airline will accept it. These examples are hypothetical and do not describe a specific product.

Connect article, category, and product without overclaiming

A reader's path may be: an article that explains a need → a category page that supports comparison → a product page that provides the specifications and purchase details. Some articles can link directly to one product; others should lead to a category or another educational page. Pick the shortest path that lets the reader make an informed next decision.

Do not force every post to point to a product. A mismatch can undermine trust, and a broken or out-of-date link creates friction. Review the destination when stock, price, compatibility, or the article itself changes. A link is navigation; it is not proof of a sale, conversion attribution, or improved search performance.

Measure link clicks separately from orders

Before changing the article, record its URL, existing destination, anchor text, date, and the reason for the edit. Test the new link in the published page on desktop and mobile. If event tracking is available, confirm that the event fires once and captures only the intended non-sensitive information.

Report distinct measures separately: article visits, product-link clicks, product views, completed orders, cancellations, and returns. A click shows that a visitor followed a link; only the store's order records can confirm an order. Attribution depends on the analytics setup, consent, identity loss, and reporting window. Do not credit a purchase to an article from timing alone. If the sample is too small, state that the result is inconclusive.

Salla editor checklist

  1. State the reader question and the article's answer in one sentence.
  2. Decide whether a product, category, or another guide is the best next page.
  3. Verify the destination URL, stock, price, specifications, and compatibility.
  4. Write descriptive anchor text that fits the surrounding sentence.
  5. Keep the article useful even if the reader does not buy.
  6. Preview the published page and test the link on a phone.
  7. Record the edit and verify any click event without treating it as an order.
  8. Recheck the link when the product or article changes.

Short-video script: Make the product link earn its place

Hook: “Does your article send readers to the right next step—or just to a product?”

Show: Put the reader's question on screen, then show two destinations: an unrelated featured item and the product that meets the article's stated criteria.

Explain: “Answer the question first. Link only when the product matches what you explained. Name the destination and mention what the shopper should verify.”

Close: “Track link clicks separately from confirmed orders. A useful link is not a sales guarantee.”

Educational carousel: From article question to product page

  1. Cover: Link the article to a relevant product, not any product.
  2. Identify intent: What question or job brought the reader here?
  3. Choose a destination: Product, category, or another guide?
  4. Check fit: Does the destination meet the article's stated criteria?
  5. Name the link: Use anchor text that describes the page.
  6. Verify details: Check availability, price, and compatibility.
  7. Test on mobile: Open the published link and inspect the page.
  8. Measure honestly: A click is a visit to a destination, not a confirmed order.

Field experiment thread: Review the article-to-product path

This is a proposed operational test, not a reported success story or observed conversion-rate result.

1/ Select a small set of articles with a clear product fit and available stock. Record existing traffic and links if reliable data exists.

2/ For each article, write the reader's question, the answer, and the next decision. Choose one destination that fits those facts.

3/ Check the URL, anchor text, product details, mobile experience, and published link. Record the starting version before editing.

4/ Change the link or its context and document the date and reason. Avoid editing several unrelated page elements at once if you want to interpret the change.

5/ Report link clicks separately from completed orders and returns, using the store and analytics sources that actually record each event.

6/ Review after an appropriate period. Note stock, price, campaigns, and sample size. If there is too little evidence to distinguish a pattern from normal variation, label the result inconclusive.

Tool-neutral prompt pack: Select a relevant product link

Prompt 1 — Match the reader's intent

Review this article and product list. Identify the reader's question and the criteria stated in the article. Recommend at most one destination that demonstrably fits, or say “no suitable destination.” Show the supporting details. Do not select an item just because its margin is higher. Article: [text]. Products: [URLs and verified specifications].

Prompt 2 — Improve anchor text

Suggest three concise anchor texts that accurately describe this destination and fit this sentence. Avoid vague “click here,” urgency, unsupported superiority, and health or sales promises. Destination details: [verified facts]. Sentence: [context].

Prompt 3 — Pre-publication review

Compare this article with the destination page. List mismatches in price, availability, compatibility, or claims; details that require manual verification; and links that may be broken. Do not assume a URL works without opening it. Article: [text and URL]. Destination: [URL].

Stop-doing swipe file: Replace the unrelated sales link

Stop: Sending a sizing guide to the store homepage.

Use: Link to the product or category that actually lists the sizes discussed, with text such as “Compare available sizes for this model.”

Stop: “Click here to buy the best one now” without identifying the item or supporting “best.”

Use: “View [product name] specifications” when that is what the destination contains.

Stop: Reporting every article-link click as a sale.

Use: Count clicks, completed orders, cancellations, and returns from their respective records.

Stop: Sending readers to an unavailable item without explaining the situation.

Use: Remove the link or explain a suitable alternative and its differences.

Hook–Value–Template: Make the product the logical next step

Hook: “If you remove the product link, can readers still use what the article taught them?”

Value: Answer the question first. Link only when the product meets a criterion you explained. Keep the article useful to readers who do not purchase.

Template: “After you choose [criterion], review [descriptive product or category link] to confirm [specification]. Check [verified limitation] before ordering.”

Illustrative example: “After measuring your available space, review [product name]'s dimensions before ordering and confirm that they suit the intended location.” This example is illustrative and does not describe a real item or observed sales lift.
