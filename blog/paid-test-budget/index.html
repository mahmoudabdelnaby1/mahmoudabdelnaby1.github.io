Set a Paid Test Budget to Buy a Decision

Define what the test can teach before deciding how much to spend.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·2 min read·العربية

A test budget should buy enough evidence to make a decision, not a promise that the campaign will “work.” Start with the outcome you need to observe and the amount you can afford to lose while learning.

Calculate a decision budget

Write down: the target action; the approximate cost range based on your own history or a clearly labeled planning assumption; the number of meaningful observations needed; and the stop condition. A simple planning model is:

Learning budget = expected cost per observed action × planned observations.

This is a planning ceiling, not a forecast. If there is no credible cost range yet, run a smaller discovery test and define what signal would justify another increment. Do not invent a benchmark from another market, season, or objective.

Worked example with hypothetical numbers

Suppose a team can allocate $600 for a two-week lead test and has a planning assumption of $30 per completed inquiry. That budget could produce about 20 inquiries at that assumed rate, but it does not guarantee 20 qualified leads. Set an additional quality check: what share receives a response, meets service criteria, and reaches the next business stage? If the landing page is not tracking correctly, stop and repair measurement before spending the full amount.

For Egypt COD campaigns, a submitted order is not the final economic result. The decision may need delivered, retained orders and return costs. For seasonal activity, record the date window and market so the test is not compared casually with an off-season period.

Define stop, continue, and change

Stop if tracking is broken, the offer is unavailable, or the spend cap is reached. Continue only if the agreed evidence is accumulating within the cap. Change the creative, audience, or page one at a time when the diagnostic points to that part of the journey. If a test produces too little data, mark it inconclusive instead of declaring a winner.
