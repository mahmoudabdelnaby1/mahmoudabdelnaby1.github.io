Outline an Article Around the Reader’s Job

Order explanations so a reader can answer, decide, act and verify progress.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·7 min read·العربية

An outline should reveal the answer and the work needed to reach it. If the first half defines familiar terms while the practical decision appears at the end, the structure serves the keyword more than the reader.

Outline around the task sequence

Write the answer in two or three sentences first. Then order sections by what the reader must know or do:

Decision → prerequisites → steps → worked example → verification → limits → next resource.

Not every article needs every section. A troubleshooting guide may start with a symptom-to-cause table; a comparison may need decision criteria before descriptions; a tutorial may need prerequisites and a reversible first step. Choose the structure based on the task, not a universal template.

Example: diagnosing a missing search page

A useful outline could be:

  1. Quick answer: determine whether the problem is access, directive, canonical, or page quality.
  2. Evidence to collect: response, robots rule, rendered meta, selected canonical, internal links.
  3. Decision path: fix the first confirmed failure, then retest.
  4. Example: one hypothetical page with a noindex directive and the correct recovery path.
  5. Limit: requesting indexing does not guarantee it.

This answers the task without adding five FAQ headings that repeat the same conclusion.

Test the outline with a reader

Give someone the headings only and ask what they expect to learn. Then ask them to find the answer to one concrete question. If the structure promises sections the article cannot support, remove them; if it hides a decision, move that section earlier. Translate the outline by reader task in Arabic rather than mirroring English headings word for word.

No magic article length

Google does not recommend a preferred word count. Expand an outline only where the reader needs a decision, evidence, worked example, or limitation. Helpful content guidance.
