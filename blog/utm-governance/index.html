Set UTM Naming Rules Your Whole Team Can Follow

Create consistent campaign tags that remain readable across teams and reports.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·6 min read·العربية

UTM tags are shared data. A naming rule is successful when two people tag the same campaign consistently and a report can group it without guesswork.

Define the fields and allowed values

Use the smallest schema the team will maintain: source, medium, campaign, and optional content or term when they answer a reporting question. Decide case, separators, date or market codes, and a controlled vocabulary. Keep campaign names readable; do not encode private customer information.

Example convention: utm_source=instagram, utm_medium=paid_social, utm_campaign=eg_summer_service_2026, utm_content=video_problem_hook. This is an illustrative pattern; choose values that match the organization’s reporting model.

Use lowercase and a fixed separator, and keep a shared builder or register as the source of truth. Do not put UTMs on internal links; they can overwrite the original acquisition source. Check redirects, QR codes, and link shorteners to ensure parameters survive.

Govern the register

Assign an owner for allowed values and a process for exceptions. Before a launch, search the register for an existing campaign name. When a name changes, document the mapping rather than silently creating a new bucket. Retire values carefully so historical reports remain interpretable.

Audit with examples

Test one link from each channel and verify the final landing URL and analytics source/medium. Look for Instagram vs instagram, inconsistent paid labels, spaces, missing campaign IDs, or parameter loss. A consistent tag improves classification but does not prove a conversion came from the campaign or that the campaign caused it.
