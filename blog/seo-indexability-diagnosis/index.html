Diagnose Why an Important Page Is Not Indexed

Separate crawl access, canonical selection, duplication and page-quality signals before requesting another crawl.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·6 min read·العربية

When an important URL is not indexed, separate the failure stages before requesting another crawl. A URL can be reachable, crawled, canonicalized elsewhere, and still not selected for indexing.

Use a page-level diagnosis

  1. Fetch: Does the URL return the expected status and content without login, redirect loops, or bot challenge?
  2. Crawl permission: Does robots.txt allow access to the page and required assets? Check the exact path.
  3. Index directive: Is there a noindex meta or header? Remember that a crawler generally needs access to see page-level directives.
  4. Canonical: Does the page declare a preferred URL? Does the search tool select a different one, such as the homepage or a duplicate language route?
  5. Content and links: Does the page answer a distinct task with enough original value, and can internal links lead crawlers and readers to it?
  6. Search-tool evidence: Inspect URL-level reports and last crawl in the verified property; compare rendered content with the intended page.

Fix the earliest confirmed issue first. Do not remove a noindex, change a canonical, or open bot access without understanding why it was set. If the page is a duplicate or low-value variant, consolidation may be better than indexing it.

A manual indexing request can ask for recrawl; it cannot force inclusion or guarantee timing. Record the diagnosis, change, and follow-up status instead of repeatedly submitting the same URL. Google’s technical requirements describe the baseline conditions.

Eligibility does not guarantee indexing

Google states that meeting technical requirements does not guarantee crawling, indexing, or serving. Diagnose the observed state and remove blockers; do not promise instant indexing. Google AI Search guide.
