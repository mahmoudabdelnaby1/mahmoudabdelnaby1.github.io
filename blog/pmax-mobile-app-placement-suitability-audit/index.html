Performance Max Mobile-App Placements: Check Suitability Before Excluding

How to review PMax mobile-app placements by separating brand suitability, campaign performance, and click quality before changing exclusions.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·6 min read·العربية

Seeing a Performance Max ad in a mobile-app placement report does not by itself prove the click was invalid or the budget wasted. Google says the placement report shows where ads appeared and impression counts, but does not include performance across all PMax channels. Use it for placement and brand-suitability review, not as a complete campaign-performance report. Google Search itself is not treated as a placement in that report. About Performance Max placement reports

Start by naming the question: is the app’s content unsuitable for the brand, or are you trying to determine whether that inventory performs? The first is a context and brand-suitability question; the second requires campaign, channel, and conversion evidence. Mixing them can lead to excluding inventory or alleging “fake clicks” from an impression count alone.

Separate placement reporting from performance reporting

Placement reports may include websites, mobile apps, and YouTube videos. They cover relevant inventory, not every PMax channel. Some Google-owned or unknown placements may appear instead of a specific publisher name. Sparse reporting does not prove ads were not served; the campaign may have delivered on channels not represented there.

Question Better evidence What the evidence alone cannot prove
Where did ads appear? PMax placement report, placement type, and impressions That all reported impressions were wasted spend
Is context unsuitable for the brand? Placement identity, content, category, and the brand’s policy That an app is fraudulent merely because it is a game
Did clicks or sales perform poorly? Campaign and channel performance, conversion diagnostics, and business records A placement’s causal effect from impression counts alone

Review the actual context before excluding anything

Inspect the app or site, its audience and content, and the brand’s written suitability rules. A category label may not describe every screen. Record the exact placement and evidence, and distinguish “unsuitable for this brand” from “low performance” or “invalid traffic.” Use Google’s invalid-click reporting and account diagnostics where relevant; do not accuse a publisher based only on a placement name or impression count.

Choose the control that matches the problem

Placement exclusions and brand controls solve different problems. Google documents brand exclusions for Search and Shopping inventory in Performance Max; that is not a general app-placement block. Review the current placement-exclusion options and their scope before applying them. Account-level negative keywords or exclusions can affect campaigns beyond the one under review, so record impacted campaigns and likely reach trade-offs first.

Short-video script: A placement report is not a ROAS report

Hook: “Your PMax report shows an app. Does that mean it wasted your budget?”

Beat 1: “The placement report shows where some ads appeared; it is not a complete report across all PMax channels.”

Beat 2: “Check the actual content for brand suitability, then use campaign data to review performance.”

Beat 3: “Choose a placement exclusion or brand control only when it matches the problem.”

Close: “Impressions alone do not prove wasted spend or fake clicks.”

Educational carousel: What a PMax app placement tells you

  1. Cover: “An app appears in the placement report. What now?”
  2. The report indicates placement and impressions, not full cross-channel performance.
  3. Identify the specific app and review the actual content.
  4. Apply the brand’s documented suitability rules.
  5. Diagnose click or conversion quality from the relevant performance evidence.
  6. Match the exclusion tool to the problem and check its scope.
  7. Measure business outcomes with campaign reporting; do not infer ROAS from placements alone.

Field experiment thread: Review an exclusion with a rollback plan

Proposed review plan, not observed results.

1/ Save the current placement report and campaign performance baseline for a defined period.

2/ Inspect the reported app or content and document the exact brand-suitability issue, if any.

3/ Check which PMax placement or brand controls are currently available in the account and what campaigns they affect.

4/ State the hypothesis narrowly: “Excluding [specific placement] addresses [documented policy concern].” Do not claim it will improve ROAS unless performance data can test that outcome.

5/ Before making an approved exclusion, record the previous setting, scope, expected reach trade-off, and rollback condition.

6/ Verify the published setting and monitor both the placement report and suitable campaign-performance evidence.

7/ Report what changed and what remains unknown; do not treat disappearance from the report as proof of better business results.

Tool-neutral prompt pack: Diagnose mobile-app placements

Evidence review: “Analyze the PMax placement report [data]. Separate placements and impressions from campaign performance. List which questions the report can answer and which require conversion or business data.”

Suitability review: “Assess the reported app or content [URL/details] against this brand policy [paste]. State the evidence and uncertainty; do not infer invalid clicks from the app category.”

Control scope: “Using current Google Ads documentation and these account controls [screenshots], explain the scope of [placement exclusion/brand control]. Identify possible campaign and reach effects. Do not change settings.”

Decision memo: “Write a short recommendation that separates brand suitability, campaign performance, and click quality. Propose one verification step and state what cannot be established from current data.”

Stop-doing swipe file: Replace placement-report assumptions

Stop: “Two games appeared, so they stole the budget.” Use: “Two app placements recorded [impressions]; we need separate cost, click, and outcome evidence.”

Stop: “Every game app is unsafe for our brand.” Use: “Review the specific content against the brand policy, then exclude only where evidence supports it.”

Stop: “Brand exclusions will block mobile apps.” Use: “Verify the documented scope; brand exclusions apply to specified Search and Shopping inventory, not all placement issues.”

Stop: “The placement vanished, so ROAS improved.” Use: “The exclusion changed the placement report; evaluate the campaign objective in the relevant performance data.”

Stop: “Add an account-level negative keyword immediately.” Use: “Check its scope and other affected campaigns first.”

Hook–Value–Template: Decide from documented context

Hook: “The placement report tells you where ads appeared; it does not tell you by itself whether you won or lost.”

Value: Separating brand suitability from performance prevents broad reactions to impression counts and preserves relevant inventory.

Copy-ready note: “We reviewed PMax placements for [period]. [Placement and type] recorded [impressions]. Review reason: [suitability/search/performance]. Evidence: [source]. Limits: [what is not shown]. Control considered: [tool and scope], potentially affecting [campaigns or reach]. We will evaluate [business outcome] using [appropriate report and period]. A placement-report change alone will not be called a performance improvement.”

Illustrative example: A report shows impressions in two apps. One contains material outside the brand’s suitability policy; the other is acceptable. Before excluding the first, the team verifies account-level scope and affected campaigns. Cost and conversions remain assessed in performance reporting; the team does not label app impressions as fake clicks.
