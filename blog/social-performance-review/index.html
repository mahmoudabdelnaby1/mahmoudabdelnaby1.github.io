Review Social Performance Without Chasing Vanity Metrics

Separate distribution, meaningful attention, audience response and downstream outcomes.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·3 min read·العربية

A social review should ask whether the post accomplished its job. Reach is distribution; it is not comprehension, trust, or a qualified inquiry.

Read performance in layers

For each post, record:

  1. Distribution: reach and impressions, with paid/organic separated.
  2. Attention: watch time, completion, or meaningful dwell where available.
  3. Response: saves, shares, substantive comments, replies, or profile visits.
  4. Action: link visits, messages received, qualified inquiries, or another defined next step.

Use the metric that fits the format; a static carousel and a video do not have identical attention measures. Compare like with like: same market, objective, placement, and a reasonable time window. Note paid spend, influencer amplification, or seasonal context.

Diagnose before declaring a winner

High reach and weak action may mean the content attracts curiosity but not the intended audience, or the next step is unclear. Low reach and strong response may mean a distribution problem despite a useful asset. A high click rate with few completed inquiries may point to the destination or offer. These are hypotheses to inspect, not labels from a dashboard.

Review a sample of actual comments and the landing path. Record what to retain, change, or test next. Do not attribute sales to a post without a reasonable link between the exposure and confirmed outcome; report correlation and attribution limits clearly.
