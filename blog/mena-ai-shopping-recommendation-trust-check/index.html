AI Helped Me Find It. Should I Trust the Recommendation? A MENA Retailer’s Proof Checklist

Separate AI-assisted product discovery from purchase trust. Use a regional survey carefully, then make product, seller, price, and policy evidence easy to verify.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·6 min read·العربية

A shopper can use an AI assistant to shortlist a product and still leave your store because the recommendation does not prove that your item is authentic, available, fairly priced, or returnable.

Tabby’s 2026 shopping survey says 43% of respondents in Saudi Arabia and the UAE had used AI to help make purchase decisions, while 30% said they fully trusted AI recommendations. The survey was conducted in November 2025 and published by Tabby, a payments company. Treat those figures as one sponsor’s survey of 20,999 people in two countries, not a universal forecast or proof that AI caused purchases. The useful editorial question is narrower: what evidence helps a shopper verify a recommendation before paying? Tabby, Ultimate Middle East Shopping Survey 2026

Separate discovery from confidence to buy

Map the journey in observable steps: a shopper asks for options, compares attributes, opens a seller page, checks the offer, and decides whether to buy. Each step needs different evidence. Discovery may expose your product; a clear product identity, current availability, final price, delivery terms, seller details, and usable returns information can help the person assess the offer. None guarantees a sale or an AI citation.

Review a sample of real product questions from search queries, customer service, and sales conversations. Remove personal data before analysis. Group them into tasks such as finding a size, comparing ingredients, checking compatibility, understanding a price difference, or confirming delivery to a city. Do not infer a person’s private traits from a question.

Make the answer verifiable on the product page

For each priority item, check that the page names the exact product and variant; distinguishes confirmed specifications from marketing language; shows stock for the shopper’s actual market; explains the full price and currency; and states delivery, warranty, refund, and seller terms in plain language. Use original photographs or diagrams when they resolve a real choice. Link claims about safety, certification, or performance to the issuer or underlying documentation. Do not say “AI-approved,” “best,” or “recommended by ChatGPT” without verifiable evidence and permission to make that claim.

Make product data consistent across the page, catalog, ads, and feeds. If the item has size, color, region, or bundle variants, let the shopper identify which offer each claim refers to. A generic category paragraph cannot answer a question about one model’s compatibility or warranty.

Measure the confidence path without pretending to read minds

Use privacy-safe events for product detail views, specification or policy interactions, variant selection, add-to-cart, checkout start, completed orders, cancellations, and refunds. Where possible, reconcile events to commerce records. These events show actions, not whether someone “trusted AI.” A voluntary post-purchase question can capture self-reported discovery source, but report response rate and do not treat it as complete attribution.

Compare product and market cohorts only when the sample and tracking are adequate. Keep price changes, stock-outs, promotion, shipping coverage, and campaign shifts in the analysis. A rise in product views after a content change does not establish that an AI answer caused the increase.

Short-video script: Discovery is not the same as trust

Hook: “An AI can suggest a product. What helps a shopper verify the offer?”

Shot 1: Show a generic recommendation, then open the exact product variant.

Shot 2: Point to the current price, availability, seller, delivery window, and return terms.

Shot 3: Open the source for one material product claim.

Close: “Make the evidence easy to check. Measure the purchase journey without claiming you can see trust.”

Educational carousel: Five checks after an AI product suggestion

  1. Cover: “The assistant found it. Is the offer verifiable?”
  2. Confirm the exact product and variant.
  3. Check the current price, currency, and stock for the shopper’s market.
  4. Read delivery, warranty, refund, and seller terms.
  5. Open the source behind a material specification or certification.
  6. Compare the same offer across the product page, catalog, and ad.
  7. Measure verified orders, cancellations, and refunds—not claimed AI influence.
  8. Takeaway: Discovery can start interest; evidence helps the shopper decide.

Field experiment thread: Find the missing proof on one product page

This is a proposed test plan, not a claim that a retailer has achieved a result.

1/ Choose one product with enough eligible visits and a clear customer question, such as a compatibility or delivery question.

2/ Review redacted search and support questions. Record the sample size and remove personal details.

3/ Find whether the answer is absent, hard to verify, stale, or inconsistent with the product feed.

4/ Add one evidence improvement, such as a variant table or a direct link to a warranty source. Keep the price and promotion stable if feasible.

5/ Define one primary user action and guardrails: completed orders, cancellations, refunds, support contacts, and page performance.

6/ Compare a suitable before/after or controlled group. Record inventory, traffic source, and other changes.

7/ Report the observed result and its limits. Do not label any conversion as AI-generated unless the source is actually known.

Tool-neutral prompt pack: Audit product proof and discovery evidence

Product-page audit: “Using these public product-page details and feed fields [inputs], identify claims that are missing, stale, variant-ambiguous, or unsupported. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions. Check price, currency, stock, seller, shipping, warranty, and returns. Cite each finding to the supplied source.”

Question grouping: “Group these redacted customer questions [sample] by the decision they help a shopper make. Show count and uncertain cases. Do not infer sensitive personal attributes or generalize beyond the sample.”

Measurement plan: “Create a test for this verified information gap [gap]. Define a shopper-focused outcome, commerce-record validation, cancellation/refund guardrails, comparison window, required sample, and an inconclusive result rule. Do not claim AI attribution from a page view.”

Stop-doing swipe file: Replace AI visibility claims with evidence

  • Stop: “AI loves our products.” Use: “We made [specific specification] and its source easier to verify.”
  • Stop: “43% of everyone shops with AI.” Use: “In Tabby’s 2026 survey of 20,999 respondents in Saudi Arabia and the UAE, 43% reported using AI to help decide what to buy; this is one sponsor’s survey, not a universal rate.”
  • Stop: “The page is trusted because the traffic increased.” Use: “Visits changed by [amount] in [period]; trust was not directly measured.”
  • Stop: “Available everywhere.” Use: “Available in [confirmed areas], subject to the current product and delivery check.”
  • Stop: “AI recommended this as the best.” Use: State the supported product attribute and provide the evidence.

Hook–Value–Template: Turn a product claim into verifiable proof

Hook: “A recommendation can start the comparison; a checkable offer helps finish it.”

Value: Exact variants, accurate availability, full commercial terms, and linked evidence reduce the work needed to assess a product without claiming a ranking or guaranteed sale.

Template: “Question: [shopper task]. Product/variant: [exact item]. Confirmed facts: [specifications and source]. Market and availability: [region, stock, timestamp]. Price and currency: [offer]. Delivery/returns/warranty: [terms and link]. Unverified points: [gaps]. Measurement: [observed events and limits]. Next action: [one evidence improvement].”

For public results, distinguish the survey’s findings from your store’s own evidence. A useful page can support both human shoppers and search systems, but no page structure guarantees that an AI service will retrieve or cite it.
