Bilingual SEO in MENA: Map Intent Before Translating Pages

A practical approach to Arabic and English search intent, localized pages, and language signals.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·2 min read·العربية

A bilingual site is not two copies of the same keyword list. People describe the same need differently across Arabic, English, dialect, and country contexts. Literal translation can preserve words while losing intent.

Build the map page by page. Record the audience and market, language, task the visitor wants to complete, one primary query hypothesis, supporting questions, and evidence behind the hypothesis. Label demand “unknown” until Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, or another reliable source validates it. Suggestions and current headings are research inputs, not search-volume proof.

Make the language experience coherent: 1. Give each genuinely localized version a stable, crawlable URL. 2. Write for the reader’s vocabulary and context; do not mechanically swap words. 3. Link corresponding language versions clearly. 4. Use reciprocal hreflang only for genuine language or regional alternatives, with consistent canonical signals. 5. Check rendered titles, headings, links, and indexability in both versions.

For Arabic, validate Modern Standard Arabic and relevant regional wording against real audience language. Avoid stuffing dialect variants into a page. A useful FAQ answers a real question naturally; it is not a synonym list.

Measure each market and language separately: impressions and clicks by page, query, and country; qualified visits where available; and business outcomes that can be verified. Do not assume a page is indexed, a translation will rank, or an AI answer will cite it. These are outcomes to observe, not promises made by markup.

Add a useful answer layer without promising AI citations

For each important question, put a direct answer near the top, then explain the conditions, examples, and limitations. Make the entity, market, currency, and date explicit when they affect the answer. In Arabic, group genuine spelling variants and local expressions during research, but do not force every spelling into visible copy. Do not mass-produce near-identical city pages; each local page needs information that genuinely helps a person in that place. Clear structure can improve understanding and extraction, but it cannot guarantee ranking or citation.
