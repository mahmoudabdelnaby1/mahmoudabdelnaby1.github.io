Compare Competitor Positioning Without Copying It

Use public competitor messaging to identify category conventions and underserved questions.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·6 min read·العربية

Competitor research should reveal the choices a buyer is comparing—not give your team permission to copy another brand’s wording. Use public evidence, label what you infer, and look for an underserved question.

Compare the buying experience

Choose a small, relevant set of alternatives: direct competitors, an in-house workaround, and doing nothing. For each, capture the public offer, audience, proof, scope, price basis if stated, and next step. Record URL and date. Do not infer delivery quality or results from a polished homepage.

Then compare by buyer task:

Buyer question What each alternative answers What remains unclear
What will I receive? Deliverable and exclusions Who does the work and in what timeframe
How do I know it works? Public case, sample, or credential Whether the example applies to my market
What will it cost? Stated price basis or quote path Which inputs change the quote

The gap column is a hypothesis to verify with interviews or sales questions, not proof that competitors fail.

Find a position you can support

Choose an unmet task that fits your actual capability and evidence. Do not claim “the only” provider or use competitor names in a comparison unless the comparison is accurate, fair, and reviewed. Avoid copying a distinctive phrase even if it describes the category well; explain your own process and limits.

Review the map when offers or evidence change. Save screenshots or notes only within allowed use, and do not access private dashboards or customer data. The output should guide a clearer offer, not create a competitor collage.
