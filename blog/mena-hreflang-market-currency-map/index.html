Selling Across Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE: Separate hreflang, Language, and Currency

A practical framework for separating language, regional targeting, and currency across Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and UAE storefronts without unnecessary URLs or search guarantees.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·9 min read·العربية

Selling the same product in Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE raises three different questions: What language does the page use? Which market is it designed for? In what currency can the customer complete the purchase? hreflang helps search engines understand the relationship between language or regional versions. It does not change currency, translate content, or guarantee that Google will show a particular version to a particular user.

Keep language and regional targeting separate from price and inventory. You may need an Egyptian Arabic version and a Saudi Arabic version, and perhaps an English version for each market. Currency needs to match across the product page, offer data, and checkout.

Decide whether localized versions are genuinely needed

Map URLs before writing annotations. For each product and category page, record its language, intended country, currency, price, inventory, shipping, returns policy, and final URL. A possible planning structure:

Market and language Example hreflang value Illustrative URL What should distinguish the version
Egypt, Arabic ar-EG /eg-ar/ EGP, delivery and returns in Egypt, relevant product details
Saudi Arabia, Arabic ar-SA /sa-ar/ SAR, Saudi shipping options, taxes, and policies
UAE, Arabic ar-AE /ae-ar/ AED, availability and delivery choices for the UAE
Saudi Arabia, English, if offered en-SA /sa-en/ English content intended for customers in Saudi Arabia

These are structural examples, not a recommendation to change existing URLs. Do not create separate versions if the content, product, and service are identical and users have no actual need for them. Do not change a published URL just to insert a country code. A URL change requires a redirect and measurement plan; it is not necessary for this method.

Google recommends using distinct URLs for language versions instead of relying on cookies or browser settings that change the same page. Google systems use visible page content to determine language; lang or hreflang alone does not translate a page. Managing multi-regional and multilingual sites.

When hreflang is useful

Use it when alternate pages are available in the same or different languages and are clearly intended for different users. Google identifies fully translated pages as a relevant case, as well as small, intentional regional variations in the same language. If the main content remains untranslated and only the template differs, Google may treat those pages as duplicates.

Each version should reference itself and the other alternates, and the annotations should be reciprocal. Use complete URLs, including https://, and supported language-region codes. The first part is a language such as ar or en; the optional second part is a region such as EG, SA, or AE. Do not use a country code without a language. An x-default URL can point to a market or language selector when there is no single targeted version for everyone. Google: Localized versions of your pages.

Do not treat hreflang as a user-facing redirect or a currency switch. Provide visible language and market choices so people and crawlers can reach the alternatives. Avoid location-based redirects that prevent access to other versions; Google notes that locale-adaptive pages may not all be crawled or indexed.

Treat currency as product and checkout data

hreflang does not set priceCurrency. For Google's product results, the documented guidance recommends a separate URL for each currency when the same product is sold in multiple currencies. Merchant Center has separate multi-country targeting and currency-conversion options, but its landing-page and checkout requirements still apply: the product data, visible page, and purchase path must be consistent for the chosen setup. Do not assume a product-feed conversion changes your organic product-page currency. Google Search Central: Multiple currencies in product results · Merchant Center: Multi-country product targeting · Landing page requirements.

Match price and currency among the page, structured data, Merchant Center feed, and checkout. Use the correct ISO currency codes such as EGP, SAR, and AED in priceCurrency; do not display an amount in one currency while submitting another. Changing only the currency symbol in the interface is not enough if the price or shipping charges still come from a different market. Check taxes, discounts, availability, and returns for the destination.

If the store theme or platform does not create a separate URL for each market or currency, verify the platform’s supported behavior before creating URL parameters or extra versions yourself. Google: Merchant listings and product structured data · Ecommerce URL structure.

Check four signals before publishing a new version

Visible content: Open the URL in a private session and verify that its language, price, and intended market are visible before user interaction. Do not rely on IP-based or automatic redirects that prevent crawlers or customers from reaching other versions; provide a market or language selector.

hreflang: Check the supported codes, self-reference, and reciprocal links among every alternate. Confirm that URLs return HTTP 200 instead of redirecting or opening an error page.

Canonical: Decide how the versions relate. Local pages with genuinely different prices, stock, or shipping may need to remain distinct. Duplicate URLs created by tracking parameters may need a stable canonical. Do not use canonical to erase a real market difference before reviewing the page and its purpose.

Product data: Compare name, offers.price, priceCurrency, and availability with the page and checkout, then validate structured data on the final page. Do not mark up a rating, offer, or availability that is not real.

Monitor language and region after launch

Use Search Console to inspect URL samples from each market, the version Google selected when the data is available, and indexing or redirect issues. Review queries and pages by country and device, but do not blame a click difference on hreflang alone; demand, price, stock, and competition may also change.

Keep an auditable map connecting each page with its market, alternates, currency, and inventory update owner. When a product is added or its price changes, update the feed and page from a shared product source, then test each version before rolling out the change.

Educational carousel: map three markets

  1. Cover: Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE: language, market, or currency?
  2. Language describes page content; ar is not a country code.
  3. Market describes the intended audience: ar-EG, ar-SA, or ar-AE.
  4. Currency must match in product data, page, and checkout: EGP, SAR, or AED.
  5. Every alternate page links to itself and the other alternates.
  6. Do not create a URL only to add a country code; confirm the need first.
  7. Takeaway: hreflang describes the relationship between versions; it does not translate or set the price.

Field experiment thread: test an alternate product-page group

This is a controlled implementation checklist, not a promise of search performance.

1/ Select a small group of products sold in more than one market. Record the source-of-truth product ID, each intended language-region, currency, and final URL.

2/ Confirm that each version differs where the customer needs it to: availability, price, shipping, returns, language, or local instructions. Do not publish a copy just to multiply URLs.

3/ Add complete alternate URLs and verify self-reference and reciprocal hreflang connections across the group.

4/ Open each URL in a normal and cookie-free session. Confirm the correct language and market are visible and that no redirect blocks another version.

5/ Check product structured data and feed data against the page and checkout for every currency.

6/ Test internal links and sitemap entries, then monitor a representative sample in Search Console over a suitable period.

7/ Fix confirmed conflicts before expanding the group. Indexing one URL does not prove the rest of the group is indexed.

Tool-neutral prompt pack: audit market versions

Alternate map

From this table [URLs, language, country, price, currency], map every alternate page. Flag unsupported codes, missing reciprocal links, and different currencies without separate URLs. Do not recommend changing an existing URL without a redirect and operational approval plan.

Product data match

Compare price, currency, and availability in the HTML, Product structured data, Merchant Center feed, and checkout capture below [data]. Show discrepancies only. Do not assume which source is correct until the commerce owner confirms it.

hreflang check

Validate this group [HTML or sitemap] for self-reference, reciprocal alternate links, valid URLs, language and region codes, and the x-default destination. Show evidence for each issue and leave uncertain items for human review.

Stop-doing swipe file: stop confusing hreflang and currency

  • Stop: “Add EG alone as hreflang and we targeted Egypt.” Use: Specify language first and region second, such as ar-EG, and make the page appropriate to that audience.
  • Stop: “hreflang will show Egyptians the price in pounds.” Use: Manage market and currency in the URL, page, and commerce data; hreflang describes alternate versions to search engines.
  • Stop: “Copying a page and swapping the country name will rank in more markets.” Use: Create a local version only when a real user or operational difference warrants it, then make it useful and test the signals.
  • Stop: Redirecting everyone by IP so they cannot reach other versions. Use: Provide visible language and market choices and make each intended URL accessible.
