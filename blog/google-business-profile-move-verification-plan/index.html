Moving Your Business? Prepare Your Google Business Profile Before Updating the Address

A practical relocation checklist for Google Business Profiles: confirm eligibility, prepare real-world evidence, follow the available verification method, and appeal only with accurate supporting documents.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·9 min read·العربية

Moving a business does not automatically mean a Business Profile will be suspended. But changing a verified address can require re-verification, and a profile may be subject to review if its information no longer matches the business's real-world operation. Treat the address change as an identity and eligibility update: confirm the new location is valid for a profile, prepare accurate records, update the existing profile through its owner account, and complete the verification method Google offers. Do not create a duplicate profile just to avoid verification.

Google says a business that moves after verification must verify again. Its video instructions vary by business type and require a live, unedited, continuous recording; the video must not expose sensitive account, tax, or ID numbers or other people's faces. Google may ask for visible evidence such as storefront signage, the nearby area, or authorization to operate, depending on the profile. A video is one available method, not a guarantee of reinstatement or of a first-try approval. Edit your Business Profile · Video verification guidance

Check eligibility and the exact profile state first

Confirm that the business still meets Business Profile eligibility rules at the new location. Does it serve customers in person at a staffed location, or travel to customers as an eligible service-area business? An online-only business without in-person service is not eligible. If customers do not visit the address, review whether the address should be hidden and the service area represented accurately. Do not use a mailbox, virtual office, or temporary location as if it were a staffed storefront.

Before editing, open the profile using the Google account that owns or manages it and record the current state: verified, verification required, disabled, or suspended. Save the exact notice, affected profile, date, and any policy or appeal link. A request to verify after an address change is different from a suspension; an address-edit rejection is also a distinct issue. Follow the action shown for that state instead of filing multiple unrelated appeals.

Prepare consistent evidence before changing the address

Make the new location operational and consistent across records before you request verification. Review the real-world business name, address, phone, website, opening status, signage, and customer-facing materials. Google advises using an accurate address and maintaining permanent fixed signage for businesses that show an address to customers. Do not add a keyword-stuffed name, claim a location before moving, or show a service area where the business does not actually serve customers.

Prepare documents that match the profile's exact business name and new address. Depending on what is available and relevant, useful records may include official business registration, an operating or business license, tax certificates, and utility bills. Keep originals and upload them only through Google's official verification or appeal flow when requested. Do not alter documents, expose unrelated account numbers, or send identity papers to a third-party “recovery agent.”

The right evidence depends on the verification method and business type shown in the profile. Do not assume that the same video checklist fits a storefront, a service-area business, and a hybrid business.

Record verification video safely and to the prompt

If Google offers video verification, read the current instructions in the profile before recording. Google currently requires a live recording captured and uploaded from a mobile device through the Business Profile flow; it says the recording must be unedited, unique, complete, and at least 30 seconds long. A pre-recorded clip uploaded later is not accepted. Confirm that the phone can provide the location, camera, and microphone access the flow requires.

Plan a route that shows only what is needed for the relevant business type. A storefront might show the nearby street or sign, then the entrance and interior. A service-area business may need to show tools, branded equipment, a vehicle, or authorization. Follow the prompt shown for your case. Keep the video continuous, do not add captions or cuts, and avoid filming customers, staff faces, private documents, account screens, tax numbers, or ID numbers. Review the clip for completeness before upload; if Google says what is missing, record a new live clip that addresses that specific point.

Do not treat “video uploaded” as “profile verified.” Google says it reviews the submission and notifies the owner of the result; the help guidance gives a review period that can take up to five business days. The timing may change, so use the current notice as the source of status. Do not keep changing name, address, category, and hours while the review is underway unless the information is genuinely incorrect and the flow requires correction.

If the profile is suspended, use the appeal path carefully

First make sure the profile follows the guidelines. Then open Google's official appeal tool while signed in to the account associated with the profile. Select the exact profile and moderation decision, read the linked policy and status, and submit only accurate information. Google lists official registration, business licenses, tax certificates, and utility bills as examples of optional supporting evidence, and says the business name and address on submitted documents should match the profile. Its current guidance warns not to create a new Business Profile for the same business while appealing. Fix suspended or disabled profiles

If the move changed the business model—for example, a storefront became a service-area operation—update the profile to represent the operation accurately rather than trying to preserve an address customers can no longer visit. If the notice concerns a restricted Google account rather than the listing, follow the account restriction process first. Keep the submission factual and concise. An appeal can be denied; no checklist guarantees reinstatement.

Field experiment thread: Rehearse the verification evidence flow

1/ Before submitting anything, assign one team member to compare the profile with the real business name, move date, new location, signage, service model, and supporting records.

2/ Have a second reviewer follow the verification prompt for the actual business type and list what the recording or documents must demonstrate. Do not record a fake or test submission.

3/ Rehearse the camera route without recording: confirm access, lighting, continuity, signage, nearby area, equipment, and authorization evidence as applicable. Remove sensitive documents and other people's faces from the planned view.

4/ Check that each document shows the profile's exact name and new address. If it does not, identify the discrepancy and resolve it through legitimate records before uploading.

5/ Submit once the real evidence matches the prompt, then track the status in Google's flow. This checklist tests completeness; it does not predict or guarantee Google's verification decision.

Hook–Value–Template: Prepare the profile around the real move

Hook: “A relocation is easier to verify when the profile and real-world evidence tell the same story.”

Value: This checklist separates address re-verification from suspension appeals, helps identify the actual profile state, and reduces avoidable name/address mismatches without promising a result.

Template: “The business moved from [old location] to [new location] on [date]. It now operates as [eligible business type]. The profile currently shows [status and exact notice]. The real-world business name is [name], supported by [records/signage]. Google offers [verification method]. We will submit [matching evidence] through [official flow], then track [status] without making additional changes unless needed.”

Example: “A staffed shop moved to a new unit and updated the existing profile. The owner verified that the sign, operating permit, utility record, and profile show the same business name and new unit. When video verification appeared, the owner followed the current storefront prompt in one live, unedited recording and kept customer faces and private numbers out of view. The profile's approval remains Google's decision.”

Represent the operation accurately, prepare the evidence the current prompt requests, and keep the verification decision separate from the business's search-performance expectations.
