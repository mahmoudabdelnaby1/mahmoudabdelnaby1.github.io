Use AI to Draft Content Without Inventing Evidence

Use AI to organize approved knowledge while making unknowns and source gaps visible.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·3 min read·العربية

Use a model to organize approved evidence, not to fill the gaps between sources with plausible-sounding facts. A clean draft should make uncertainty visible to its editor.

Separate source, inference, and unknown

Give the drafting workflow a bounded source pack. For each sentence with a factual claim, record one of three labels:

  • Supported: a source directly confirms the statement.
  • Inference: a conclusion drawn from several facts; show the reasoning and keep the language qualified.
  • Unknown: no approved source supports it; ask for evidence or remove it.

A sample prompt rule might say: “If the source pack does not state the price, availability, result, or policy, write [VERIFY] and do not estimate it.” The reviewer should be able to trace every [VERIFY] item to an owner.

Example: describing a marketing method

A source says a GA4 report lists sessions by source/medium. That supports an instruction to inspect the report. It does not support a claim that “AI traffic increased 30x” or that the report proves which tool influenced a sale. Those require different data and a transparent method. Keep the explanation useful by showing the fields to inspect and the attribution limit.

For Arabic versions, preserve the distinction between an observed fact and an inference. Do not strengthen a cautious English phrase during transcreation. Check names, dates, currencies, and platform terms against the same source pack.

Review before release

Verify links, quotations, changing claims, and calculations. Ask a second reviewer to sample factual sentences and attempt to reproduce the conclusion. If the evidence is missing, update the source pack or publish a narrower answer; do not let the model invent a citation.

Carousel draft: the claim ledger before the polished post

1 — Hook

A confident sentence is not evidence.

2 — Extract

List every number, comparison, guarantee, and policy claim.

3 — Attach

Record the source, date, exact support, and scope beside each claim.

4 — Resolve

Mark unsupported claims as questions for the owner; do not fill gaps from model memory.

5 — Draft

Write only after the evidence survives review.

6 — Close

Use AI for structure; keep evidence and approval with people.

Fact-check generated claims

Google advises reviewing AI-assisted content for accuracy, quality, and relevance before publishing. Keep the source ledger and human approval described above. Guidance on generative AI content.

Model-neutral prompt pack: Use AI to Draft Content Without Inventing Evidence

These prompts work with any text assistant and can also be run manually. Use sanitized inputs; do not grant the model publishing or final decision authority.

Build the procedure:

Turn this principle into operational steps for “Use AI to Draft Content Without Inventing Evidence”: Give the drafting workflow a bounded source pack. For each sentence with a factual claim, record one of three labels: Specify inputs, owner, measurement source, and risks. Separate facts from assumptions. Invent no figures or outcomes; ask for clarification when information is missing.

Audit the draft:

Check the plan against this source requirement: - Supported: a source directly confirms the statement. - Inference: a conclusion drawn from several facts; show the reasoning and keep the language qualified. - Unknown: no approved source supports it; ask for evidence or remove it. Identify unsupported claims, vague actions, and privacy risks. Suggest a correction without adding facts.

Repurpose it:

Rewrite the approved procedure for the intended audience and format. Use this application only if relevant: A source says a GA4 report lists sessions by source/medium. That supports an instruction to inspect the report. It does not support a claim that “AI traffic increased 30x” or that the report proves which tool influenced a sale. Those require different data and a transparent method. Label hypothetical material, distinguish it from measured results, and do not copy the article verbatim.
