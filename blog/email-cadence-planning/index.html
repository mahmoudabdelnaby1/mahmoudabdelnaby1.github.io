PRACTICAL GUIDE · Email marketing

Set Email Cadence Around Value and Expectations

Choose a rhythm the audience understands and the team can sustain.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·7 min read·العربية

Email cadence is a promise about how often a person should hear from you. Set it from subscriber expectations and the value you can sustain—not a calendar quota.

Plan against the content supply

List the updates that genuinely change a subscriber’s decision: new product information, useful education, service notices, or a requested digest. Estimate how often these updates occur and whether one email can combine them. If nothing meaningful changed, skipping a send is a valid choice.

Write the cadence in the signup experience and preference center using plain terms such as “monthly” or “only when there is a major update.” Do not say “weekly” if the team cannot support useful weekly content. Set a maximum frequency across overlapping campaigns and define how transactional or service messages are handled separately.

Watch fatigue and value together

By cohort, review delivered messages, meaningful clicks or replies, unsubscribes, complaints, and downstream actions. Compare cohorts that received different cadence only when the audiences and offers are comparable. Opens alone are a weak basis for increasing frequency.

If complaints rise after a new series, inspect the signup promise, overlap, and relevance before blaming the audience. If a monthly digest is more useful than separate notices, consolidate. Revisit cadence when the content supply or subscriber expectations change.
