A CRO Test Needs a Falsifiable Hypothesis and a Stop Rule

Design experiments around a specific friction, measurable outcome, guardrails, and decision rule.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·2 min read·العربية

“Make the page convert better” is a desired outcome, not a test hypothesis. A useful experiment names the observed friction, proposed mechanism, audience, and signal that should change if the explanation is right.

For example: “For visitors arriving from X, replacing the unclear shipping paragraph with a concise delivery summary may reduce checkout exits because the page hides decision-critical information.” It is testable and could be wrong.

Before launch, define: - Primary metric: one outcome closest to the page’s job, such as completed checkout or qualified form. - Guardrails: cancellations, refunds, errors, lead quality, or support contacts that must not worsen materially. - Eligibility: audience, device, language, and traffic sources included. - Instrumentation: event definitions, variant assignment, deduplication, and data-quality checks. - Decision rule: sample or duration logic, practical effect threshold, and response if evidence is inconclusive. - Stop rule: technical errors, harmful outcomes, or broken user experience.

Do not keep peeking and declaring a winner whenever a chart turns green. Small samples are noisy, and simultaneous changes obscure cause. If traffic is insufficient, use usability sessions, support questions, or a staged rollout and label the conclusion accordingly.

Keep MENA calendar effects inside the test design

A test that compares different weeks may confuse a page change with payday, weekends, Ramadan, or a major sale period. When possible, run variants concurrently and record the market calendar that could affect demand. Do not span a major seasonal regime change with one simple comparison. Evaluate the outcome closest to business value—such as net margin or completed qualified actions—while keeping cancellation, payment failure, and support contacts as guardrails. The dates and thresholds must fit the real traffic and sales cycle.
