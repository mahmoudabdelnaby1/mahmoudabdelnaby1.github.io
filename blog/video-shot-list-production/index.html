Create a Video Shot List That Protects the Message

Plan the minimum footage needed to show the user problem, process and evidence clearly.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·6 min read·العربية

A shot list gives every shot a job. It prevents the common failure of returning with attractive footage but no usable proof, transition, or alternate framing.

Turn the story into coverage

For each approved story beat, specify purpose, location, subject, action, framing, movement, spoken line or on-screen text, evidence needed, and alternate take. Note continuity requirements such as wardrobe, screen state, product orientation, and light. Assign an owner for permissions, props, and data privacy.

Film hard-to-recreate material first: a real workflow, permissioned environment, product result, or location-dependent scene. Capture close-ups, clean backgrounds, room tone, and a clear voice take where useful. Leave safe space for platform overlays and captions.

Example: reporting workflow

Capture a sanitized starting screen, the user action, any real processing step, the resulting state, and the report’s date range. A second take can show the result at readable mobile scale. Do not expose customer records just because they look credible.

Before wrap, match footage to the outline and verify releases and music rights. Record missing coverage while reshooting is still possible. In edit, check that cuts preserve causal order and do not hide manual work or alter the apparent result.

Deliver files with names, version, rights status, and gap notes. A complete list improves readiness; it does not guarantee performance.

Educational carousel: Create a Video Shot List That Protects the Message

  1. Cover: A shot list should protect the message, not just fill the camera roll.
  2. The failure: Attractive footage is unusable when the key action, proof, or transition is missing.
  3. Write one row per story beat: Beat | viewer takeaway | location | subject/action | framing/movement | line or overlay | proof | alternate.
  4. Example — reporting workflow: Starting screen → user action → actual processing → resulting state and date range.
  5. Make proof readable: Capture a phone-safe alternate. Use sanitized or sample data; credibility does not justify exposing customer records.
  6. Protect continuity: Record wardrobe, screen state, product orientation, light, permissions, props, and a named owner.
  7. Run the wrap check: Match every required beat to a clip. Log gaps, unclear rights, and any shot that changes the apparent cause or result.
  8. Takeaway: Schedule the hard-to-recreate, permissioned footage first; it is the hardest coverage to recover later.

Stop-doing swipe file: Create a Video Shot List That Protects the Message

Stop writing shot requests that describe only the look. “Get a nice dashboard close-up” does not say what the shot must prove.

Stop using an unexplained before/after. A cropped starting screen followed by a result can imply a change without showing the action or time period.

Rewrite the shot request like this:

Beat: Show the reporting workflow. Viewer should understand: what changed and when. Capture: sanitized starting screen, actual user action, real processing step, resulting state, and report date range. Alternate: phone-readable framing. Guardrails: hide customer records; confirm permission; keep the sequence intact.

Stop leaving the reshoot decision until the edit. At wrap, map every required beat to a clip and record each missing shot while the location, people, and setup are still available.
