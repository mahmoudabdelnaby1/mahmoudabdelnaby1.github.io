Interview Customers to Find Useful Content Questions

Use short, non-leading interviews to learn vocabulary, uncertainty and decision barriers.

Mahmoud Abdelnaby·6 min read·العربية

An audience interview is for learning how a person handled a recent task, not asking them to approve your article idea. Past behavior gives richer clues than “Would you read this?”

Interview one recent decision

Recruit people who actually faced the task. Explain the purpose, ask permission to take notes, and avoid sensitive details. Use a short sequence:

  1. “Tell me about the last time you tried to [task].”
  2. “What did you do first, and what happened next?”
  3. “Where did you hesitate, search, or ask someone for help?”
  4. “What words did you use to describe the problem?”
  5. “What finally helped you decide or finish?”

Ask for examples rather than opinions about hypothetical content. Do not coach the person toward the service or solution you want to promote.

Turn notes into a content decision

Record the task, context, exact phrases with permission, observed workaround, barrier, and what remains uncertain. Remove names and private details. Compare patterns across roles and markets; one interview can reveal a useful question but cannot establish how common it is.

For Arabic MENA research, note the country and language register. Do not combine an Egyptian phrase and a Saudi use case into one supposedly universal insight. Verify repeated themes against search queries, support records, or service data before investing in a series.

The output is a brief for one answer or tool, not an article title chosen from the most quotable sentence.
