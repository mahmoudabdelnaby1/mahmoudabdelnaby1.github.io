Marketing Mix Modeling: Data Readiness & Decision Brief

Use this framework to assess whether marketing and business data can support a responsible marketing-mix analysis, then communicate what the model can and cannot say.

Use this guide as a practical framework, then verify changing details against current primary sources before making a decision.

01

When to use it

A team is considering a marketing-mix model, comparing a model output with an experiment, or preparing a budget decision that depends on modeled channel contribution.

02

Prepare these inputs

Dated outcome series; media exposure and spend by channel and time; geography where relevant; pricing, promotions, distribution and other business controls; known tracking or taxonomy changes; experiment evidence; data owner and decision horizon.

03

A practical workflow

  1. 01

    Name the decision, geography, time unit, outcome and channels before selecting a model.

  2. 02

    Inventory data coverage, definitions, missing periods, granularity, spend/exposure consistency and structural breaks. Document the reason for each exclusion or transformation.

  3. 03

    Check whether the dataset contains the channel and business variation needed for the intended question; do not treat a long time series alone as proof of identifiability.

  4. 04

    Record priors, controls, carryover/saturation choices, validation design and any experiment calibration as assumptions to review.

  5. 05

    Report estimates with uncertainty and sensitivity; distinguish attributed performance, modeled contribution and experimentally estimated incrementality.

  6. 06

    Translate findings into a decision range and a follow-up measurement plan. Revisit the model when data, channel mix or business conditions change.

04

What the toolkit contains

A data dictionary, readiness checklist, transformation log, model-assumption register, validation plan, and decision-disclosure template. It is a planning and review framework, not a fitted model or budget recommendation.

05

Review boundaries

Model quality depends on data, design and assumptions. Use current official method documentation and a qualified measurement reviewer; do not present modeled estimates as causal proof or guaranteed outcomes.
