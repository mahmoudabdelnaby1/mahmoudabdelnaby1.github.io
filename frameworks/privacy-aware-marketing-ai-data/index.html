Privacy-aware marketing AI & customer data

Reduce exposure before using customer or CRM data with AI: classify, minimize, control access, document purpose, and verify vendor handling.

Read the source framework ↗All six frameworks ↗
01

When to use it

Use before using customer records, support transcripts, CRM exports, lead lists, or behavioral data in an AI tool or automated marketing workflow.

02

What you need

  • Data fields, source, purpose, audience, and intended model/vendor
  • Access roles, retention/deletion controls, transfer locations, and contract terms available
  • Applicable internal policy and review contact for the relevant market
03

Workflow

  1. 01

    Map the flow

    Record where the data originates, where it moves, who can access it, and what decisions use the output.

  2. 02

    Minimize before transfer

    Remove fields not needed for the task; avoid direct identifiers and sensitive details unless explicitly authorized and necessary.

  3. 03

    Check the tool and terms

    Verify retention, training use, access, deletion, subprocessors, region, and contractual controls from current vendor documentation.

  4. 04

    Choose a safer method

    Use synthetic or aggregated examples where possible; otherwise use approved environments, role controls, and documented safeguards.

  5. 05

    Review output use

    Check whether profiling, targeting, or automated decisions introduce additional review or notice requirements.

  6. 06

    Record and govern

    Log purpose, data categories, reviewer, vendor, retention, incident contact, and review date; test deletion and revoke access when no longer needed.

04

Output

A data-flow record, minimum-field dataset, vendor and purpose review, access/retention controls, and documented approval decision.

05

Boundaries

This framework does not determine legal basis or compliance. Requirements vary by jurisdiction, data, purpose, and vendor; get qualified privacy/legal review.

06

How it fits existing work

This is a data-handling framework, not a CRM integration or legal-compliance promise; it complements existing measurement and messaging playbooks.
