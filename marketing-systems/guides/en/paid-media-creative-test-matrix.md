# Paid Media Creative Testing Matrix

Plan controlled creative tests that separate audience, message, format, and offer hypotheses.

## When to use it
Use this matrix when a team has many creative ideas but needs a readable test sequence and decision rules.

## What you need
Business outcome; baseline; audience; creative inventory; budget; test duration; attribution and reporting definitions.

## Workflow
1. Write the hypothesis and one variable to learn about.
2. Group variants so each comparison answers a specific question.
3. Separate controllable creative dimensions: audience tension, message/claim, format, opening visual, offer, and CTA. Change a limited number at once so the result remains interpretable.
4. Check asset eligibility, audience overlap, placements, landing-page match, rights, and tracking before launch. Do not infer platform algorithm behaviour from a creative pattern.
5. Set a budget and observation window consistent with expected volume.
6. Predefine decision thresholds, minimum evidence, and external factors to annotate.
7. Record results by delivery, qualified business outcome, and cost; note which comparisons were not sufficiently powered.
8. Record the next decision without calling noise a winner.

## Practical output
A test matrix, naming plan, decision log, and learning summary.

For creator/video concepts, include distinct hypotheses such as demonstration, objection response, use case, and problem framing. Treat hook, first-frame action, spoken line, proof shown, subtitles, CTA, and localization as explicit creative fields rather than claiming any one formula is proven.

## Scope and limits
The matrix does not guarantee a winning ad. Interpret results with adequate volume, stable tracking, and business-quality outcomes.
